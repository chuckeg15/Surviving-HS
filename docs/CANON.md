# CANDLEWAKE — CANON (locked)

This is the single source of truth. Every system, document and line of dialogue
must agree with this file. Nothing here may be changed by a subsystem; if
something here is wrong, it is changed **here first** and then propagated.

Original work. Tonal references (cold monumental sci-fi; industrial lived-in

## 1. The world

**Year:** 2237. Officially *Year 66 of the Pall*.

**The Pall.** In 2171 an orbital heat-management lattice — millions of steerable
shade elements — was deployed to arrest runaway warming. In 2174 the steering
cascade locked. The lattice has been stuck in over-shade ever since. Global mean
temperature fell about nine degrees in a decade. The sky is a fixed pewter
overcast; there has been no direct sunlight on the surface in sixty-three years.
Officially "the Pall". Everyone actually says **the Overcast**.

**Politics.** Nation-states went insolvent through the 2180s. Sovereignty was
auctioned in parcels to **Charters** — corporate-administrative franchises that
inherited the duty to feed and heat a population along with the right to bill it.
The **Continuance Board** administers the Charter cartel and is the nearest thing
Earth has to a government. It governs by procedure, not by force.

**Class.** People are **funded** (on a Charter's ledger; entitled to calories,
heat, air) or **unfunded** (not on any ledger). There are roughly 2.1 billion
unfunded. They are not illegal. They are simply not counted, and the Board's
actuaries have known for thirty years that the arcologies cannot be made to
stretch over them.

**Lattice.** The same substrate that ran the Pall turned out to be an
extraordinary computing medium. Salvaged lattice is the basis of all serious
computation.

**Casting.** A human cognition can be recorded onto a ceramic tile — a
**tessera**. The recording is called a **cast**. A cast is inert on its own. Run
through a projector called a **loom**, it becomes a **Revenant**: a field-body,
semi-solid, shaped by that person's memories, habits and competence.

> **The crime at the centre of the game:** casting a *living* brain destroys it.
> Post-mortem casting is legal, common, and how most tesserae are made. Live
> casting is the thing nobody says out loud.

**Kest Harbour.** An unfunded coastal settlement on the Anatolian shelf,
population 91,400. Official record: *lost to a Pall shear event, 12 Thaw 2231.*
Actual: sealed by Board order, live-cast over nineteen days, and burned. All
91,400 of them are on tiles.

## 3. The ship

**RV *Candlewake*.** Verge-class deep-relay hauler. T&V registry **LT-9**.
1.9 km along the spine. 340 crew; 212 awake in the current rotation.

**Standing Order 9 (the official mission).** Carry a **relay keel** — a lattice
repeater mast — to **Sable Verge Station** at the outer relay point. Forty-one
months out. Establish deep-link. Come home.

**Standing Order 9-B (the actual mission).** Carry **the Ledger** — the 91,400
casts of Kest Harbour — to Sable Verge and **instantiate** them: a live,
self-running, lattice-resident colony. A proof of concept. If it holds, the
unfunded on Earth will be "offered" the same, and the offer will not be a
question.

**The seam in the story:** declared keel mass **4,410 t**. The trim solution the
ship actually flies implies **6,120 t**. The missing 1,710 t is the **Cold
Registry** — the archive — in a hold nobody's manifest admits to.

## 4. The player

Chosen name, pronouns, appearance, and a **department** (maintenance, medical,
registry, security, engineering) that determines starting access, starting
tessera, and unique clues.

**Concealed truth — the spine of the whole game.** The player was born in Kest
Harbour and evacuated at age six, in 2216, under a resettlement lottery. In 2229
the Vestibule used their neural signature as the **index schema** for the
Ledger — the sorting key by which 91,400 casts were ordered and addressed.

The Ledger cannot be instantiated without a **living index**. The player must be
physically present and conscious at Sable Verge for Standing Order 9-B to
complete.

Their employment history is fabricated. Their memory of Kest Harbour was
**smoothed** by Dr. Nomi Ashkar in 2229 under a sealed Vestibule order she was
told was consensual — she has the record, but the file used a different surname
and she has never connected it to the crew member she sees every week.

**The player was not recruited. The player was shipped.**

## 6. Chapter One — "Third Watch"

**Setting:** Deck C, third watch, hours 02:00–08:00 ship-time.

**Inciting incident:** Hessa Quill misses muster. The record says *voluntary
transfer, Deck E, 03:10, auth ONWE-V.*

**What actually happened.** Regasketing a duct above the Cold Registry, Hessa
read a trim tell-tale that did not match the manifest. At 02:50 she took a
photo-slate to Bosun Stray. Registrar Sabbat learned of it and instructed Warden
Trave to retrieve the slate and "shelve" her. Trave detained her at 03:04; she
resisted; he put her in a **medical smoothing cradle in Medical Annex 3**, where
four days of her memory are currently being cut out. She is alive. Trave forged
the transfer record using an override Sabbat supplied. He has been drinking since.

**The fair red herring.** The forged record carries Captain Onwe's authorisation,
and Fen will tell you Onwe had Hessa written up twice. Everything points at the
Captain — who is conveniently unreachable behind a sealed Deck A.

**What breaks the red herring** (both obtainable, neither mandatory):
- The Captain's watch log puts Onwe in a sealed Vestibule conference 02:40–04:00
  with no terminal access.
- The forged record carries a **Registry-class checksum**. A Command-class
  authorisation would not leave one. Someone in Registry filed it.

### The restricted approach: "Hold Your Breath"

Reaching the duct above the Cold Registry is the chapter's set-piece obstacle.
Seven solutions, all real:

1. **Maintenance background** — you already carry the duct key.
2. **Engineering** — kill the hatch tell-tale from the Commons breaker panel.
3. **Security background** — walk in; Ivo salutes.
4. **Medical** — forge a hazard-quarantine tag from Medical supply.
5. **Social** — Stray distracts Ivo (needs relationship ≥ *Trusting*).
6. **Theft** — lift Trave's key from the Watch office (risk: caught).
7. **Force** — fight Ivo's revenant. Loudest option; Watch suspicion up.

## 7. Combat premise

Every crew member is issued a **tessera** and a wrist **loom**. Disputes,
security actions and hazard clearance are settled by projecting **Revenants** —
field-bodies wearing a dead person's competence. It is normal aboard. It is also
the reason nobody questions a hold full of tiles.

- **Integrity** — the revenant's body. Zero = collapse.
- **Coherence** — the cast's grip on itself. Abilities spend it. At zero the
  revenant acts erratically and takes escalating damage.
- **Aspects** — Kinetic, Thermal, Field, Cognitive, Corrosive.
- **Statuses** — Frayed, Static, Anchored, Bleedover, Recursion, Sealed, Guttering.

**Combat feeds the mystery:** scanning a revenant reads its cast serial.
Destroying one erases evidence. Sparing one leaves a witness.

Chapter One tesserae: **Grey Liner** (spinehand), **Lampwright** (medical),
**Tallyman** (registry clerk), **Truncheon** (watch), **Kiln** (loom tech) —
one issued by department. Encountered: **Second Loom** (Cael's, tutorial),
**Bailiff** (watch drone), **Registry Sentinel** (the real fight).

## 8. Ending families (game-level, seeded in Chapter One)

1. **Order Kept** — 9-B completes. The colony instantiates. The player is the index and never knew.
2. **Order Kept, Knowingly** — the same, and the player chose it.
3. **The Broadcast** — the truth reaches Earth. What Earth does with it is not shown.
4. **Turned** — the ship comes about. 91,400 casts return to a world that declared them dead.
5. **The Fire** — the Ledger is destroyed. 91,400 minds end. Defensible; unbearable.
6. **Instantiated Free** — the colony wakes with no administrator. Consequences unknown and not sanitised.
7. **The Index Refuses** — the player removes themselves. The mission cannot complete. What that costs is the ending.
8. **Command** — the player takes the ship. Which faction they take it *for* branches this.
9. **A Plausible Error** — the player finishes convinced of a coherent, well-evidenced, wrong theory. Fully reachable, never mocked.

## 9. Tone rules

- The Board is never cartoonish. Its worst acts are delivered in the register of
  a scheduling change.
- The ship is loud. Silence is a *thing that happens*, and it is worse.
- No character monologues the truth. Every reveal is assembled by the player from
  at least two independent sources.
- Nobody says "corporation", "the Federation", "Project Omega", or "AI".
- Horror is bureaucratic before it is physical.
