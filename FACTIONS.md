# CANDLEWAKE — FACTIONS

**Status:** derived from `docs/CANON.md` §2. The five factions, their wants,
methods and blind spots are frozen. History, structure, aboard-ship presence,
membership, reputation rules and mechanical grants are addition.

**Cross-references:** `NARRATIVE_TRUTH.md` §C for knowledge boundaries;
`CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` for the people; `ENDING_MATRIX.md` for how standing is read
at the end.

---

## 0. The shape of the system

Five factions, five reputation tracks, and no morality axis. The tracks do not
sum to a constant — it is possible to be well regarded by three at once and
possible to be loathed by all five.

### Reputation bands

| Band | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Marked** | −3 | Actively worked against. Doors close; people project at you. |
| **Suspect** | −2 to −1 | Watched. Offers withdrawn, prices raised. |
| **Unknown** | 0 | Default for four of the five at the start of the game. |
| **Noted** | +1 to +2 | You exist to them. Minor grants. |
| **Held** | +3 to +4 | You are useful. Real grants, real obligations. |
| **Ours** | +5 | You are theirs. The largest grants, and they will spend you. |

**T&V starts at +1 (Noted)** for every player, because every player is a T&V
contract employee. Every other track starts at 0.

### Three system rules

1. **Standing is a record of behaviour, not a currency.** There is no way to buy
   reputation and no faction quest-giver dispensing tasks. Standing moves when
   the player does something the faction can observe and cares about.
2. **Observation is required.** An act nobody witnesses moves nothing. This is
   why O4 — say nothing, keep the slate — moves no track at all, and why it is
   simultaneously the coldest and the most flexible ending state.
3. **Two factions can hold you at once.** Dual standing is normal (see §6) and
   is the source of most late-game pressure: the Ninth Watch and the Board both
   holding the player at *Held* is a survivable and extremely uncomfortable
   position, and the game does not resolve it for you.

## 1. The Continuance Board

> **Wants:** the unfunded problem solved without famine or revolt.
> **Method:** convert surplus population into lattice-resident colonies that need
> no calories. Genuinely believes this is mercy.
> **Blind spot:** cannot conceive that being counted is not the same as being
> alive. *(canon)*

### History

Constituted around 2190 to administer the Charter cartel after the sovereignty
auctions. It has no army, no police and no territory. It has a schedule, a
ledger, and the exclusive authority to say which Charter owes what to whom, and
that has proved sufficient. It governs by procedure — canon §1 — which in
practice means it governs by being the only body that knows where anything is.

By 2210 its actuaries had concluded that the arcologies could not be made to
stretch over 2.1 billion unfunded. The conclusion was filed. It has been filed
for twenty-seven years. Around 2222 an internal paper proposed *lattice-resident
continuance*, and the Board did what the Board always does with a proposal it
cannot yet execute: it scheduled it.

### Structure

Committees, not ranks. There is no Chairman and no headquarters. Authority is
attached to *portfolios* and portfolios are attached to procedure, so a
Registrar in a portfolio outranks a Charter director outside it. This is why
Sabbat, who commands nobody, can shelve a crew member on a ship he does not own.

### Methods

- Amendment rather than order. Things are *revised*, *closed out*, *brought into
  line*.
- Access as the primary instrument. The Board rarely denies; it schedules.
- Categorisation as violence. Being moved from one column to another is the
  Board's characteristic act, and Kest Harbour is that act at scale.
- **It never lies about small things**, which is what makes its large lies
  survivable.

### Aboard the *Candlewake*

One person: **Registrar Ilm Sabbat**. He has an office on Deck B adjacent to
Ship's Registry, a Command-class override, and a Vestibule protocol key. He also
has, informally, the Watch — Trave takes his instructions without either of them
ever calling it a chain of command.

Board presence aboard is therefore *one man, two keys, and a habit of being
helpful*. It is proportionate to its power.

### Reputation with the Board

| Gains | Loses |
|---|---|
| Filing correctly, at all, about anything (+1) | Being the subject of an incident report (−1) |
| **O2 — filing with Sabbat (+3, and asset status)** | Fighting a custodial revenant (−1) |
| Bringing an irregularity to the Board before anyone else (+1) | O3 — Ninth Watch involvement (−3, immediate) |
| Declining to act on evidence when procedure says wait (+1) | Being named by another crew member as an organiser (−2) |
| Producing a complete, sourced document (+1) | Destroying ship property, including revenants (−1) |

### What the Board offers and denies

| Offers | Denies |
|---|---|
| Credential tiers — T2 at *Noted*, standing T2 at *Held*, T4 read at *Ours* | Nothing, ever, explicitly. The Board's denial is a delay. |
| Protection from the Watch: an asset is not detained | At *Suspect* or below, every request is processed "in the normal course", which means never |
| Documents nobody else can obtain, including manifest annexes | |
| At *Ours*: Standing Order 9-B, read once, in a room, with Sabbat present. He will explain it as mercy and he will be persuasive. | |

**The Board's mechanical signature is that it gives you what you asked for.** It
never blocks the player. It grants, and the grants accumulate into a position
from which the only remaining choice is the one the Board wanted.

## 2. Trestle & Vance (T&V)

> **Wants:** to be paid and to avoid liability.
> **Method:** owns the *Candlewake*'s hull and every crew contract. Cynical, not
> ideological. Will sell anyone out on paper.
> **Blind spot:** assumes everything is negotiable. *(canon)*

### History

A Charter-era salvage and haulage firm that survived the insolvencies by owning
hulls rather than territory. It has no politics. It has never refused a contract
on moral grounds and has never been asked to. It carries the *Candlewake* under
registry **LT-9** and it carries Standing Order 9 in good faith, because
Standing Order 9-B was not shown to it and T&V did not ask.

T&V's institutional position on 9-B, if it ever learns of it, will be that the
liability sits with the Board. It will be right, and it will be paid.

### Structure

Contracts, all the way down. Every person aboard except Sabbat and Rask is a
T&V contract, including the Captain. The company's presence aboard is not a
person — it is the **contract terms**, which are consulted more often than any
officer.

### Methods

- Paper. Everything is a variation, a rider, an indemnity.
- Deniability by delegation: T&V does not do things, T&V contracts for things.
- Genuine, unsentimental competence. The ship is well-found and well-maintained
  because a badly maintained ship is a claim.

### Aboard

- **Captain Verity Onwe** — master, T&V contract, and the person T&V would sell
  first.
- **Warden Callix Trave** and the Watch — the Watch is a T&V function, which is
  why Trave keeps reaching for regulation: regulation is his employer.
- **Bosun Anneke Stray** — T&V bosun, and the cover is perfect because the job
  is real.
- **Cael Oduya, Fen Bellweather, Dr. Ashkar,** and every rating aboard.

### Reputation

| Gains | Loses |
|---|---|
| Completing watch-list work (+1 per chapter, passively) | Damaging ship property (−1) |
| Preventing an incident that would be a claim (+2) | Injuring crew, including via revenant combat (−1 each) |
| Reporting a hazard through the proper channel (+1) | Theft from a T&V space (−1 if detected) |
| Backing the Captain in any dispute (+1) | Being detained (−1) |
| | O3 lockdown: the whole crew loses standing, player −2 |

### What T&V offers and denies

| Offers | Denies |
|---|---|
| Tools, stores, and the quartermaster's ear | Nothing ideological. T&V will not help you and will not stop you. |
| **Legal cover** — at *Held*, an incident becomes a variation rather than a charge | At *Suspect*, contract terms are enforced strictly, which means the player's movements are logged and their watch list doubles |
| Loom maintenance and modification through Cael | At *Marked*, the contract is suspended and the player loses department access entirely — the single most punishing standing state in the game |
| At *Ours*: the hull. T&V is the only faction that can hand the player a ship, which is why it matters for ending family 8. | |

## 3. The Vestibule (Bureau of Deep Registry)

> **Wants:** to be the ones who understand casting.
> **Method:** custodians of the science. Internally split: some think the Ledger
> is a rescue, some know it is a mass grave.
> **Blind spot:** mistakes precision for ethics. *(canon)*

### History

Chartered around 2207, five years after the first stable post-mortem cast, to
hold the science before a Charter monetised it. It has been broadly successful
and broadly complicit. It wrote the consent frameworks, the depth conventions,
the serial schema, and the index theory that made the Ledger addressable — and
it wrote all of them beautifully.

The internal split is not a conspiracy and has no name. It is a disagreement
about whether a lattice-resident colony is a population or a recording, and it
runs through departments, marriages and individual heads. Rask is split down
the middle by himself.

### Structure

Bureau class, then research grade. Credentials are absolute and portable: a
Vestibule protocol tag opens a medical cradle on a ship the Vestibule does not
own, and nobody queries it, which is exactly how Hessa ended up in Annex 3.

### Methods

- Documentation before intervention.
- The sealed order — a Vestibule instrument that carries authority without
  content. Ashkar's 2229 order was one. C12's annex is one.
- Consent frameworks that are technically satisfied.
- **No coercion, ever.** The Vestibule has never forced anyone. It has arranged
  for people to be in situations where the correct form was available.

### Aboard

- **Tibold Rask** — researcher, Deck B, honest log, no idea what he is inside.
- **Dr. Nomi Ashkar** — not Bureau, but Vestibule-credentialed for smoothing.
  She holds a Vestibule instrument on a T&V contract, which is precisely the dual
  position the Vestibule prefers to create.
- **The Vestibule research suite** on Deck B, and the sealed conference room in
  which Onwe spends 02:40–04:00.
- Sabbat holds a Vestibule protocol key without being Vestibule, which is a
  professional irregularity Rask would report if he noticed.

### Reputation

| Gains | Loses |
|---|---|
| Producing a complete, correctly sourced observation (+1) | Asserting something you cannot support (−1) — the Vestibule track punishes *sloppiness*, not opposition |
| Scanning rather than destroying a revenant (+1 per unique cast) | Destroying a revenant carrying an unscanned cast (−1) |
| Handing over a cast serial or provenance record (+1) | Forging a Vestibule tag and being caught (−2) |
| Engaging Rask on the internal split honestly (+2) | Damaging a tessera (−2). This is the Vestibule's only real anger. |

### What the Vestibule offers and denies

| Offers | Denies |
|---|---|
| Tessera identification: what a cast was, who it was, when it was taken | Provenance. They will tell you *what* and never *where from* below *Held*. |
| Loom capability: depth control, coherence recovery, the ability to read a cast without projecting it | At *Suspect*, credential revocation, which closes T4 permanently for that run |
| At *Held*: the serial schema, which is what makes `KH-` legible | |
| At *Ours*: **the index theory** — the reason a Ledger needs a living index. This is the single most important grant in the game and it is the direct precondition for ending family 7. | |

## 4. The Recurrence

> **Wants:** the Ledger instantiated — as scripture.
> **Method:** a faith holding that the Pall was a correction and that flesh is a
> draft.
> **Blind spot:** wants the right outcome for reasons that make it monstrous.
> *(canon)*

### History

Emerged in the 2210s among unfunded populations and casting-industry workers —
the two groups with the most direct experience of being converted from one
category into another. Its founding intuition is simple and awful: the Overcast
was not a failure, it was an edit; the world was overwritten because the draft
was bad; and a cast is not a copy of a life but the corrected version of it.

It has no clergy and no buildings. It has **celebrants**, who are ordinary
people who observe, and **the Reading**, which is the practice of speaking a
dead person's cast serial aloud on the anniversary of their taking.

### Structure

Cells of two to six. No hierarchy. A celebrant is whoever is currently
observing. This makes it impossible to decapitate and nearly impossible to
detect, and it also means it cannot do very much.

### Methods

- Presence. Recurrence members are near things, and they wait.
- The Reading, which sounds like a woman muttering numbers at a bulkhead and is
  in fact a devotional act.
- **They never lie, and they never explain.** A celebrant asked a direct question
  answers it and offers no context.
- They will not fight for the Ledger. They will remove obstacles from its path
  and consider that sufficient.

### Aboard

Canon: *small aboard, but not nothing.* **Proposed** (see open questions):

- **Anouk Delisle**, steward — a practising celebrant. She prays over the keel
  bulkhead because she believes it is a reliquary, and it is. She reported
  Hessa's remark to Trave because a rumour about the weight of a reliquary is a
  matter for authority. She has no idea what she did.
- **Bezhi Oram**, cook — lapsed, embarrassed, and still knows the Readings.
- Two unnamed ratings in the fourth watch.

The Recurrence's aboard-ship presence is therefore **four people who will never
take an action**, one of whom already caused the entire chapter. That is the
correct scale: the faith's power in Chapter One is one sentence spoken to the
wrong man out of reverence.

### Reputation

| Gains | Loses |
|---|---|
| Treating a tessera as a person in dialogue (+1) | Destroying a tessera (−3). The only −3 single act in the game. |
| Speaking a cast serial aloud after scanning (+1) | Referring to casts as data, cargo or property in a celebrant's hearing (−1) |
| Sparing a revenant (+1) | Advocating the Ledger's destruction where a celebrant hears (−2) |
| Attending a Reading (+2) | |

### What the Recurrence offers and denies

| Offers | Denies |
|---|---|
| Movement. Celebrants are stewards, cooks and ratings; they are everywhere and nobody watches them. At *Noted* they will carry a message; at *Held* they will carry an object. | Any material help. They have no keys, no credentials and no money. |
| Interpretation: at *Held*, a celebrant will tell the player what the serial schema *means* devotionally, which is wrong about the science and right about the number of people. | Any assistance with ending family 5. They will actively obstruct destroying the Ledger. |
| At *Ours*: the Reading of the Ledger — a celebrant speaks a serial the player has scanned and knows the name attached to it, which is the game's most direct route to understanding the scale. | |

## 5. The Ninth Watch ("hollowmen")

> **Wants:** the ship turned around and the truth broadcast.
> **Method:** cross-Charter labour network. Organised, patient, willing to use
> people.
> **Blind spot:** not saints — they will spend the player. *(canon)*

### History

A cross-Charter labour network that formed in the 2220s out of the ship-and-yard
trades, which are the only workforce that moves between Charters and can
therefore compare notes. The name is a shift joke: there are four watches, and
the ninth is the one nobody is rostered for. "Hollowmen" is what the Charters
call them and they have adopted it without affection.

They have organised strikes, four of them successful. They have never organised
a mutiny in deep space and they know the difference.

### Structure

Cells with a **keeper** who knows one member of the next cell up. Aboard the
*Candlewake* there is one cell of four in the awake rotation, and Stray is its
keeper. She knows one name ashore. She has never met the person that name
belongs to.

### Methods

- Slow recruitment. Six weeks of ordinary conversation before an ask.
- Documentation theft, badly executed — Stray has a manifest page she cannot
  read, which is the Ninth Watch in miniature: they get the evidence and lack the
  expertise.
- Work stoppage as the primary weapon; a 1.9 km ship cannot be flown by its
  officers.
- **Instrumental honesty.** Stray tells the player she will use them, out loud,
  because it works better than concealment and because she is not ashamed of it.

### Aboard

- **Bosun Anneke Stray** — keeper.
- **Spinehand Ostrow Kell** — her second, and the only one who knows the full
  local network. *(proposed)*
- Two members in the awake rotation, unnamed, deliberately.
- **Hessa Quill** — *not* a member. A sympathiser who went to Stray because
  Stray is the bosun and that is the correct channel. Stray's guilt about
  Hessa is that she treated a safety report as an intelligence asset.

### Reputation

| Gains | Loses |
|---|---|
| Sharing evidence with Stray without being asked (+1 per clue) | Filing anything with Sabbat (−2) |
| **O3 (+4, ally)** | **O2 (−5, permanently Closed)** — the player has handed the Board an organiser's name |
| Covering for a crew member against the Watch (+1) | Reporting a work stoppage (−2) |
| Refusing a Board grant in Stray's hearing (+2) | Accepting Sabbat's patronage visibly (−2) |
| Taking a personal risk for a rating with no rank (+2) | Backing the Captain over the crew (−1) |

### What the Ninth Watch offers and denies

| Offers | Denies |
|---|---|
| People. The largest and only *manpower* grant in the game: at *Held*, four crew will do a thing at the same time in four places. | Credentials. They have none and cannot get any. |
| Movement without record — they know which hatches are unlogged and which watchmen do not look | Anything requiring Board or Vestibule authority |
| At *Held*: the stolen manifest page, which is useless until the player has Vestibule-level schema knowledge, and devastating after | At *Suspect*: they stop talking to the player entirely, which is quieter and worse than hostility |
| At *Ours*: the crew. Ending families 3, 4 and 8 all require Ninth Watch standing at *Held* or better, because none of them can be done by one person. | |

## 6. Dual membership and cross-pressure

Who belongs to more than one, and why it matters.

| Character | Primary | Secondary | The tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ilm Sabbat** | Continuance Board | Holds a **Vestibule protocol key** without Vestibule membership | A professional irregularity. Rask would report it if he noticed. It is the mechanism by which Hessa was put in a cradle. |
| **Nomi Ashkar** | T&V (contract physician) | **Vestibule-credentialed** for smoothing | Her employer cannot see what her credential lets her do, and her credential-holder is not aboard to supervise. She is accountable to nobody, which is why she is the way she is. |
| **Anneke Stray** | **Ninth Watch** (keeper) | T&V (bosun) | Her cover is a real job she is good at, and doing it well is what makes her trusted enough to organise. Every hour spent being a good bosun is an hour spent recruiting. |
| **Callix Trave** | T&V (Watch is a T&V function) | Takes **Board** instruction informally | Neither he nor Sabbat has ever called it a chain of command, which is exactly why it worked. Trave's employer is T&V; his orders come from a man with no authority over him. |
| **Verity Onwe** | T&V (master) | **Board**-briefed on 9-B | She holds a Board secret against her own crew's interest and calls it protecting them. |
| **Anouk Delisle** | T&V (steward) | **Recurrence** celebrant | She serves the ship and worships its cargo, and the second loyalty is invisible to the first. |
| **Tibold Rask** | **Vestibule** | — | Single. Notably the only senior figure who belongs to exactly one thing, and the only one who keeps an honest record. That is not a coincidence and the game should not underline it. |
| **The player** | T&V (+1 at start) | Whatever they build | The only character who can end the game at *Held* with three factions at once, and the only one for whom that is a problem rather than a resource. |

### Cross-pressure rules

1. **The Board and the Ninth Watch are mechanically opposed.** Gains in one at
   *Held* or above apply an automatic −1 to the other. No other pair does this.
2. **The Vestibule and the Recurrence are not opposed but are mutually
   embarrassing.** A player at *Held* with both finds each will discuss the other
   only in the third person and with distaste, and neither will withdraw a grant.
3. **T&V is compatible with everything** until the player does something that
   creates liability, at which point T&V is compatible with nothing.
4. **No faction ever asks the player to renounce another.** They ask for acts.
   The renunciation is a consequence the player works out afterward.

## Open questions for the lead

1. **The Recurrence's aboard-ship membership** is unnamed in canon ("small
   aboard, but not nothing"). This document proposes Delisle as a practising
   celebrant, Oram as lapsed, plus two unnamed fourth-watch ratings. Confirm or
   replace. The choice matters because Delisle-as-celebrant is also the leak
   mechanism in `NARRATIVE_TRUTH.md` §A.2.
2. **Ninth Watch cell size aboard.** Set here at four in the awake rotation
   (Stray, Kell, two unnamed). Canon says "organised, patient" and gives no
   number. Four is small enough that the manpower grant feels earned and large
   enough that a coordinated action is possible. Confirm.
3. **Does T&V ever learn about 9-B?** This document assumes not, in Chapter One,
   and assumes T&V's eventual position is that the liability sits with the Board.
   Whether T&V ever becomes an active player — rather than a set of contract
   terms — is a Chapter Four question that affects ending family 8's T&V branch.
4. **Faction standing at the point of the ending matrix.** `ENDING_MATRIX.md`
   reads standing directly. The numeric bands here (−3 to +5) are proposed and
   should be checked against the actual save-state design before implementation
   hardens.
5. **The Board's −1 for "fighting a custodial revenant".** This makes the
   loudest Hold Your Breath route (force) cost Board standing, which is intended.
   But it means a combat-forward player is quietly locked out of the Board's late
   grants, including the 9-B read at *Ours*. That may be correct. It should be a
   decision.
